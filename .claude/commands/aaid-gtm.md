## Step 1 — Interview

Ask these questions **one at a time** before generating the GTM plan.

1. What product are we taking to market? One-sentence description.
2. Who is the Ideal Customer Profile? (B2B: company size, industry, tech stack; B2C: demographics, behaviour)
3. Who would the customer use if your product didn't exist? (the real competitive alternative)
4. What is your unique attribute — what can you do that no competitor does meaningfully well?
5. What pricing model are you leaning toward? Any constraints (investor expectations, competitor anchoring)?
6. What is your primary acquisition channel hypothesis? (content, paid, community, sales-led, PLG)
7. What is your target launch date or timeline? Which phase are you in now (pre-alpha / beta / GA)?
8. What budget tier are you working with for the first 6 months? (bootstrapped / seed / Series A+)

Once all answers are collected, say "Got it — generating the GTM strategy now." and produce the document below.

## Step 2 — Output

### Ideal Customer Profile
**Firmographic/Demographic**: company size, industry verticals (top 3 ranked by fit), geography, adjacent tools they use.
**Psychographic/Behavioural**: what pain is acute enough to trigger active search? What does success at 90 days post-purchase look like? What causes switching away from incumbents?
**Negative ICP**: 3 explicit disqualifiers — segments that waste sales resources.

### Positioning Statement
April Dunford positioning canvas:
1. Competitive alternatives (what customers do without you)
2. Unique attributes (features only you have or do meaningfully better)
3. Value (business outcomes those attributes enable — tied to ICP pain)
4. Target customers (specific ICP segment where value resonates most)
5. Market frame of reference (category you compete in or are creating)

Geoffrey Moore synthesis: *For [target] who [problem], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [alternative], [product] [differentiator].*

### Pricing Model Options
3 options, full analysis each: structure, entry price, scale pricing, pros/cons, best-fit stage.
Conclude with: **Recommendation** — which to start with and under what conditions to revisit.

### Channel Strategy
**Owned**: website/SEO keyword clusters, PLG virality loops, email nurture cadence.
**Earned**: press/analyst targets, community (forums, Slack, subreddits), partnership types.
**Paid**: platforms, audience targeting approach, initial budget guidance, retargeting strategy.
Per channel: CAC tier (Low/Medium/High) and time-to-results (Immediate / 3–6 mo / 6–12 mo).

### Launch Phases
Phase 0 (Alpha/Closed Beta): duration, cohort size, success gate.
Phase 1 (Private Beta): acquisition method, feedback loop, success metrics.
Phase 2 (Public Launch): launch moment strategy, onboarding conversion target, 30/60/90-day milestones.
Phase 3 (Growth & Scale): expansion motion, international sequence, partnership activation.

### KPIs per Phase
4–6 KPIs per phase: metric name, definition, target value, measurement frequency.

### Risk Register
6–10 GTM risks. Per risk: ID (R-XX), description, probability (H/M/L), impact (H/M/L), mitigation, contingency. Must include ≥1 competitive risk and ≥1 execution risk.
